# Keymeter product

## Register

product

## Users

Customers who received an Enterprise-Shared RunningHub API key from a seller. They want to paste their key and quickly see what remains and what each job cost. The account owner manages one persistent billing connection privately.

## Product Purpose

Show the individual key allowance and actual per-job charges without substituting the shared wallet balance. All keys in the connected account work automatically.

## Brand Personality

Clean, modern, technical. Keymeter uses a black canvas, charcoal panels, white primary actions, and precise monospace data.

## Anti-references

No illustrative balances, sample results, marketing hero, dense explanations, repeated setup steps, or decorative dashboard controls. The user explicitly asked to remove unnecessary text and examples.

## Design Principles

- Put the key lookup first and use one primary action.
- Show financial figures only after a successful lookup.
- Present the remaining allowance and job charges directly.
- Keep help and secondary history collapsed until needed.
- Keep owner security and connection renewal separate from customer lookup.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Retain visible labels, keyboard navigation and focus, readable contrast, responsive tables, accessible error/loading states, and reduced-motion support already present in the project.

## Top-ups and owner settings

A visible pricing card shows all six USD packages beside the checker on desktop and below the balance area on mobile. Its bottom “Top up my key” action opens a contact-only dialog with the seller's Facebook/Telegram links. Prices are stored in the app database and can be edited by the authenticated owner from billing settings. Contacts never receive a key automatically. Customer accounts, direct top-up orders, payment collection, and seller notifications are deferred until separately requested.

The standalone owner verifies a fixed setup email and receives a 64-character owner key once. Later sign-ins use the owner key and an email code; forgotten keys can be replaced after email verification. Email changes require reauthentication and verification. The email delivery service is configured privately; there are no displayed test codes or password-only fallbacks.
